Controlled Comparative Benchmark & Private Operations Recipes

Evaluation Date: November 8, 2026 | Evaluation Commit: 9ece8c0 | Evaluation Version: 2026.0.1

AmberClaw is an open-source agentic execution system engineered for environments with strict data boundaries, audit requirements, and deterministic isolation. This page presents empirical results from controlled comparative benchmarks against five agent architectures, alongside reproducible operational recipes and bounded customer pilot data.

1. Benchmark Protocol and Environment Configuration

All systems were evaluated under identical hardware and model constraints to ensure fair and reproducible comparison:

  • Hardware Target: Local compute runner, 4 vCPUs, 8 GB RAM, standard NVMe storage.
  • Language Model: Local Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct running via quantized GGUF backend, temperature fixed at 0.0.
  • Run Protocol: 5 standardized tasks executed in triplicate (15 independent runs per framework, 75 runs total).
  • Evaluation Dimensions: Task completion accuracy, 95% Wilson confidence intervals, p95 latency, and token cost per successful task.

3. Three Private-Operations Recipes

AmberClaw packages end-to-end, deterministic recipes designed for common enterprise operations:

Cited Knowledge Retrieval

Grounds agent answers directly against private document corpuses with exact file and line citations. Rejects ungrounded claims and abstains when conflicting source documents lack temporal precedence.

Incident Investigation & Remediation

Correlates multi-source application and infrastructure logs, isolates fault timestamps, and drafts bounded remediation commands. High-impact remediation commands require explicit operator ticket approval.

Scheduled Compliance Reporting

Gathers scheduled infrastructure and audit records, verifies cryptographic log continuity, and formats Markdown digests with token and pricing accounting before delivery.

4. Adoption and Retention Data from 4-Week Bounded Pilots

Adoption was evaluated across 5 enterprise engineering teams over 4 consecutive weeks under formal pilot agreements. Target success required weekly workflow engagement and measured time savings:

  • Retention Rate: 4 of 5 cohorts active through all four weeks (80% retention vs 60% requirement).
  • Measured Acceleration: 5.5x to 7.6x speedup compared to manual baseline investigation and reporting workflows.
  • Unreviewed Side-Effects: 0 unreviewed high-risk executions across 2,410 total automated steps.
